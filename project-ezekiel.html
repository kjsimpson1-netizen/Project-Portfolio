Autonomous Robot

Overview

I worked together with 3 other Engineering Physics students to design an autonomous robot. The challenge was to create a robot that could autonomously navigate a surface while following a line of electrical tape on the ground, while completing a series of tasks that are outlined here.

My Role

During the project, I was primarily responsible for the mechanical design and manufacturing of the robot. I designed the robot online using OnShape (the model is linked here ), and then I manufactured the parts using a variety of methods, such as 3D printers for more complicated parts, as well as 6mm particle board which was cut with a laser cutter. There were also 6mm aluminum sheet metal that was cut with a waterjet for the arm.

Control Details

The robot primarily ran on an ESP32 microcontroller, which communicted via Rx and Tx pins to a Rasperry Pi 4. The primary role for the ESP32 was to get feedback from the variety of sensors on the robot, and then use those to control the drive train and the mechanical arm. The Raspberry Pi was solely used to run machine vision on the Pi Cam to identify teletubbies.

Sensors

The robot had a variety of sensors for live input and feedback. The line following was done with 2 IR sensors on the underside of the robot. The robot arm had a metal detecting coil with a metal detecting IC to detect if metal was inside of the rock. The front of the claw also had an integrated ToF sensors to sweep for rocks, for less error.

Arm Control

The robot arm itself was controlled via a set of 30kg-cm servos at the base of the arm, and a regular servo in order to drive the claw. The two base servos were used to move the boom and the dipper of the arm, and they were positioned using an inverse kinematics algorithm specific to the lengths of the boom and dipper in order to translate physical locations into relative angles. The base angle of the arm was controlled using a potentiometer located at the base, and a DC motor to drive the rotation. This feedback loop was controlled via self tuned a PID controller.
